# Submissions Reference

This page is a cross-reference. Every submission in this reference is described in the context of a specific position — that is where you will find the setup, the finishing mechanics, and the details that make it work. This page collects all the entries in one place so you can quickly find where each submission is covered.

Use this page as a lookup table, not a learning tool. For the full technique, go to the page listed.

## 11.4 Americana

The americana is a shoulder lock applied from a mounted or side control position, rotating the opponent's arm in the opposite direction from the kimura.

### Entry

The primary entry is from the shoulder clamp sweep in butterfly guard ([The Shoulder Clamp Sweep](positions/05-open-guards.md#the-shoulder-clamp-sweep-to-your-left)). Both sweeps — to the left and to the right — land you in mount with your opponent's right arm splayed out on the mat. Keep your arm under their arm, chest pressure pinning it to the mat. When they try to pull their arm back and bring their fist toward their head, it feeds into the americana: your arm is already under their arm, bring your other elbow near their ear, grip their wrist, and apply the rotational pressure.

The americana can also be attacked from mount by rolling one of their arms down, but this is grueling against a tight defense ([Making Them React](positions/07-mount.md#71-making-them-react)).

## 11.6 Guillotine

See [The Guillotine](positions/09-front-headlock.md#94-the-guillotine) for the complete treatment. Key finishing principle repeated here:

**Do not arch back.** The guillotine works by closing the aperture between your body and your choking arm. Close it with gravity, not arm strength. Position their head between the ground and your body, move sideways across their neck, and let your weight close the space. Dig your chin into the back of their neck and scoop forward.

## 11.7 Anaconda and D'Arce

See [The Anaconda Choke](positions/09-front-headlock.md#92-the-anaconda-choke), [The D'Arce Choke](positions/09-front-headlock.md#93-the-darce-choke), and [the D'Arce from side control](positions/06-side-control.md#the-darce-choke) for the complete treatment.

**Anaconda:** Arm goes around their head first, then around their arm. Roll through to finish. Key detail: drag their arm across with a gable grip and pull them forward *before* the roll.

**D'Arce:** Arm goes under their armpit first, threading across their neck. Finish from the side without rolling. Key detail: sprawl on your far hip to bring your chest down on their head, crushing arm and head together.

**When the anaconda does not finish:** Do not switch to a guillotine. Instead, grip their tricep, get to your knees, and transition to side control or north-south.

## 11.8 Head and Arm Choke

The head-and-arm choke (also called the arm triangle) traps your opponent's head and one arm together, using your arms in a figure-four to compress the neck.

### From Mount (Primary)

Walk their elbow up to their ear by lifting and pushing forward repeatedly. Link a gable grip behind their head. Slide your knee under their shoulder into S-mount. Transition to the armbar from there (the head-and-arm is the setup; the armbar is the finish). See [The Head and Arm Choke to Armbar](positions/07-mount.md#the-head-and-arm-choke-to-armbar).

### From Front Headlock (the arm triangle guillotine)

From the gable grip with their arm dragged across — or after winning the arm with the step-over: switch grip so one arm holds their tricep and the other grips your own wrist. Drop, throw your leg over their back, and pull back with head and arm inside. See [The Arm Triangle Guillotine from Front Headlock](positions/09-front-headlock.md#the-arm-triangle-guillotine-from-front-headlock).

### Finishing, Switching Sides, and the Hip Ankle Lock

Full ankle lock finishing mechanics (guillotine wrap, choking up on the ankle, the 180-degree turn, bridge and pull) and the technique for switching to the opposite leg when they escape are covered in [The Ankle Lock from X-Guard](positions/05-open-guards.md#the-ankle-lock-from-x-guard). ### Estima Lock

From the top when your opponent has their foot on your hip in reverse de la Riva: lean down and trap their ankle in the crook of your waistline ([Open Guards](positions/05-open-guards.md#54-dealing-with-de-la-riva-and-reverse-de-la-riva-from-the-top)). Get a guillotine grip on the ankle and rise up. Very fast — be aware of belt-level legality.

### Toe Hold

From reverse de la Riva: reach around their ankle with your far arm, grip the other side of their foot, and apply rotational pressure ([Open Guards](positions/05-open-guards.md#54-dealing-with-de-la-riva-and-reverse-de-la-riva-from-the-top)). Typically not legal at white and blue belt.

### Checkmate Position

From the saddle (entered by stepping between their legs and pivoting 90 degrees, then falling back; see [The Saddle / Reverse Saddle Entry](positions/05-open-guards.md#the-saddle--reverse-saddle-entry)): if you can grab their other leg and bring it over the top of their trapped leg, wrap your arm around it. Both legs are trapped and overlapping. **Figure-four your legs** so your heel is not exposed to heel hook attacks.

## 11.10 Heart Choke

The heart choke is a shoulder lock that uses the opponent's own arm pinned across their chest. It works from half guard top and side control.

**Setup:** Create pressure into their forearm brace until they push back hard. Drive your shoulder across their brace, pinning their arm against their chest with your chest weight. Reach your far arm around their head, establish a figure-four (arm clasps bicep, hand over their eyes). The pressure falls on their shoulder joint or pectoral.

**From half guard:** Use the pressure to transition to mount (sprawl to free your trapped leg). Re-establish the figure-four in mount.

**From side control:** Drop to your hip, step over their head to pull their head across their body. When they push your leg off with their arm, go around the arm, return to side control with their arm trapped across their body. Drive your knee into their hips for twisting pressure, then transition to mount.

**When they turn to relieve pressure:** Transition to the gift wrap and back control. See [The Heart Choke](positions/04-half-guard-top.md#46-the-heart-choke) and [The Heart Choke from Side Control](positions/06-side-control.md#the-heart-choke-from-side-control).

## 11.11 Rear Naked Choke

The rear naked choke is the primary submission from back control. Your choking arm slides under their chin and wraps their neck. Your other hand grips your choking arm's bicep. Your choking hand goes behind their head. Squeeze your elbows together.

The challenge is getting the arm under their chin against active defense. Patience and positional control matter more than speed. Keep the seatbelt tight, maintain your hooks, and work the arm in over time.

See [The Rear Naked Choke](positions/08-back-control.md#the-rear-naked-choke).

## 11.12 North-South Choke

A shoulder choke from north-south: one arm over their shoulder, hands gable-gripped, your shoulder driving down onto their neck. It comes up during the carving transition from side control.

| Position | Where |
|----------|-------|
| North-south, their right side (arm-out) | [Side Control](positions/06-side-control.md#the-carving-system) |
| North-south, opposite side, when they clasp their hands | [Side Control](positions/06-side-control.md#the-carving-system) |

**Finishing principle:** get as far from their chest as you can, toward the top of their head, so the neck is stretched and exposed and all of your weight goes through your shoulder into it. Staying high lets their chest or shoulder carry the weight.

**Why it is easy to lose:** they drive up into you; they turn their head into you and shrimp; or they chin-strap the back of your neck and reverse you. Treat it as an opportunistic finish during a transition, not a position to sit in.
